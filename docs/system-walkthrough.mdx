# How Vektorprogrammet works

Vektorprogrammet connects volunteer university students with schools that need mathematics tutoring.
The software helps people recruit volunteers, organize teams, plan school service, and record what actually happened.

This guide starts with those human needs. Each layer then explains the machinery underneath them.
You do not need to understand Effect or PostgreSQL before you start.

> **Three different states:** Legacy PHP still runs production. The native replacement has local operational and migration acceptance. Production cutover remains a separate, unauthorized step.
>
> This guide describes the native replacement, not a claim about a deployed replacement. [STATE.md](../STATE.md#current) owns current status.

## 1. The whole system in one picture

There are two web applications and one modular native backend. PostgreSQL stores the durable business facts.
Private receipt files have separate storage.

Read the arrows as responsibility boundaries, not four separate network calls.
An SDK is client code, not another running service. Frontend server loaders can call the backend too.

- **Homepage:** public information and entry points, including recruitment.
- **Dashboard:** authenticated work for volunteers, coordinators, organizational leaders, and finance roles.
- **Backend:** current authority, business decisions, persistence, and delivery programs.
- **Storage:** durable records, history, queued work, and private file bytes.

“Modular” means that different parts own different decisions. It does not mean that each part needs its own server.
Organization owns appointments. Placements owns school assignments and service workflows. Economy owns reimbursement claims.

**The central rule:** one fact has one owner. Other parts use that owner's contract rather than maintain competing copies.

## 2. People, relationships, and promises

### One person can have several relationships

A student can be an assistant, team member, coordinator, and receipt owner at the same time.
These are separate relationships, not a single role field that changes meaning between screens.

| Fact                      | What it means                                                     | What it does not prove                      |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Person                    | The stable identity of a human                                    | A password or permission                    |
| Account                   | Credentials and access to authentication                          | Current organizational authority            |
| Profile                   | Contact and personal information                                  | A grant of access                           |
| Appointment               | A position in an organizational unit during an effective interval | Assistant affiliation or global authority   |
| Volunteer affiliation     | The person's assistant relationship with a chapter                | A school assignment                         |
| Placement                 | A recurring school assignment for a semester                      | Attendance on a particular date             |
| School-service commitment | A dated interval, required demand, and scheduled assistants       | That the promised service actually happened |

These distinctions prevent shortcuts that corrupt meaning.
An imported historical leader does not automatically receive current leadership authority.
A roster with two names does not prove that two assistants attended a visit.

### Recruitment creates separate decisions

An application starts a recruitment process. It does not create a school assignment.
Interview scheduling, the applicant's response, assessment, and an onboarding invitation retain their own states and evidence.

Account claim or linking establishes access to the person's identity.
Volunteer affiliation establishes the chapter relationship. Placement establishes the school assignment.
A returning volunteer still needs the relevant affiliation and placement decisions.

The system therefore does not use one vague “accepted” flag to imply all later steps.

### Planning is not completion

A coordinator records school demand and reviews an assignment proposal.
Roster confirmation freezes the plan and queues notifications. It does not record attendance.

A dated commitment then names a school, a service interval, the required number of assistants, and their scheduled assignments.
An absence or acknowledged substitute changes the dated situation without rewriting the original semester plan.

The terminal outcomes have different meanings:

- **Completed:** attendance evidence meets the required demand for the service interval.
- **Cancelled:** an authorized cancellation closes the commitment with its evidence.
- **Unfulfilled:** the outcome records unmet demand rather than invent success.

An elapsed end time proves none of these outcomes.

### The supporting workflows remain distinct

Organization manages departments, teams, and appointments.
Schools manages the school records that planning uses.
Economy records reimbursement claims, review decisions, and separate settlement evidence.
Communication uses authorized recipient projections and delivery processes.

Content, events, and surveys have their own contracts. They are not one large “school visit” object.
They are also not default migration gates merely because the legacy site contains them.
An active operational obligation still needs an explicit disposition.

## 3. The screens collect intent

The browser presents facts and collects a person's intended action.
It does not own the authoritative state of an appointment, placement, or claim.

The current applications use React Router. The dashboard also embeds Foldkit applications for stateful operational workflows.
React Router still owns routing and server rendering. This is not a completed, repository-wide Foldkit rewrite.

Within a Foldkit workflow:

1. A **Model** holds the screen state, including drafts and pending requests.
2. The **view** renders that model.
3. A **Message** describes an event, such as a button press or server response.
4. **update** chooses the next model and any commands.
5. A **command** performs an effect and returns a result as another message.

This makes a failed save, stale data, and a pending request explicit.
The dashboard's custom elements mount and dispose independent Foldkit runtimes. They do not share one hidden React workflow state.

For example, “save in progress” is a screen fact. “The roster is confirmed” is a server fact.
A spinner cannot prove that a command succeeded. A stale screen cannot preserve authority that the server revoked.

> **A hidden button is not a security boundary.** A forged request must still fail on the server.

Sources: [frontend state](architecture.md#frontend-state), [dashboard entry](../apps/dashboard/app/entry.client.tsx), [school workflow](../apps/dashboard/app/foldkit/schools/main.ts), [custom element lifecycle](../apps/dashboard/app/foldkit/schools/elements.ts).

## 4. The HTTP contract separates the browser from the server

A screen sends an operation, not an arbitrary database edit.
The operation has input schemas, response schemas, known failures, and protocol rules.

The canonical application contract lives in `packages/http-api`.
OpenAPI and consumer artifacts derive from that contract.
The SDK creates typed operations from the external API definition and decodes responses.
It is not a second place to define business meaning.

| Boundary                       | What crosses it                                                          | What stays hidden                                    |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Human browser to server        | Action, credentials, inputs, revision, and retry identity where required | Database tables and internal implementation          |
| Machine to server              | Service identity, capability, scope, and supported credentials           | Provider-specific authentication plumbing            |
| HTTP adapter to domain service | Business command or query, actor, domain values, and explicit failures   | HTTP parsing and response encoding                   |
| Domain service to persistence  | Reads, constraints, locks, and transactional writes                      | Public UI layout and transport details               |
| Delivery program to provider   | A committed effect and its delivery identity                             | Authority to invent the underlying business decision |

Effect Schema decodes values at external and durable boundaries.
TypeScript types alone cannot establish that incoming JSON or a stored value is valid.

Two protocol concepts solve different problems:

- **Revision precondition:** “Apply this edit only if the fact still has the version I observed.”
- **Idempotency identity:** “This request is a retry of the same intended command, not a new decision.”

A stale edit must not overwrite newer work.
A repeated command must not produce a second business effect.
Reuse of the same retry identity for a different request is a conflict.

Better Auth owns separate credential routes under `/api/auth/*`.
The application has separate external and internal API roots. The public OpenAPI excludes the internal root.
An internal URL alone grants no authority.

Sources: [API root](../packages/http-api/src/api.ts), [SDK construction](../packages/sdk/src/effect-client.ts), [interface contracts](architecture.md#interface-contracts), [encoding](architecture.md#boundary-encoding).

## 5. Identity answers who; authority answers what

**Authentication** establishes who presents a credential.
Better Auth manages human credentials and sessions. Native account identity connects to the canonical Person.
New passwords use Argon2id. Supported legacy credentials can upgrade after successful sign-in.

**Authorization** decides whether that actor can perform this operation on this resource now.
The decision uses current relationships, capabilities, ownership, scope, and time.

A department leader does not automatically control another department.
A historical appointment is evidence of the past, not a current grant.
Import of an account does not prove current ownership of its email inbox.

For protected commands, the backend resolves current authority inside the command transaction.
In the shared native command path, authorization precedes retry lookup.
Therefore, a previously successful retry identity does not preserve access after revocation.

Machine access has a separate principal and credential policy.
For example, the receipt approval-queue read supports a service bearer token with current scoped grants.
That does not grant machines blanket access to all operations.
An internal receipt-evidence operation still uses its specified Person credential policy.

Network isolation adds a restriction. It does not replace business authorization.

Sources: [identity and authority](architecture.md#identity-and-authority), [authentication engine](../packages/database/src/auth-engine.ts), [password policy](../packages/database/src/password-codec.ts), [receipt HTTP operations](../apps/backend/src/receipt/http.ts).

## 6. Business services own the decisions

A domain command expresses intent: confirm a roster, record an absence, or attach settlement evidence.
It is not permission to edit any column in a database row.

Portable contracts define the business values, states, rules, failures, and observations.
Concrete implementations read current facts and apply the transition inside the transaction.

Two Effect concepts explain the structure:

| Concept | Plain meaning                                                                | Example                         |
| ------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Service | A typed dependency: what this part of the program can ask another part to do | Execute a Placements command    |
| Layer   | An implementation of that dependency and its lifecycle                       | Placements backed by PostgreSQL |

The program's dependencies and possible failures remain visible in its types.
A pure local calculation remains a function. It does not need a service wrapper merely to follow a pattern.

Portable contracts live in `packages/domain`; their PostgreSQL implementations live in `packages/database`.
Placements exports its contracts as `@vektorprogrammet/domain/placements` and its database Layer as `@vektorprogrammet/database/placements`.
Its service owns complete commands and queries; it is not only a schema collection.

The Placements HTTP adapter handles transport concerns and calls the service inside the native command transaction.
The service holds the department lock across its precondition, transition, audit, history, and outbox writes.

This boundary permits changes to SQL or internal structure without changing what a caller's operation means.
It does not imply that every older module already has the same boundary. Substitutes still has direct backend-to-database calls.

Sources: [domain services](architecture.md#domain-services), [Placements service](../packages/domain/src/placements/service.ts), [Placements HTTP adapter](../apps/backend/src/placements/http.ts), [domain exports](../packages/domain/package.json), [database exports](../packages/database/package.json).

## 7. Transactions protect facts; workers deliver effects

### One database decision

PostgreSQL stores native business facts.
A command transaction can commit the changed state, history, audit, retry receipt, and queued external work together.

Database constraints protect invariants that PostgreSQL can express.
Locks protect decisions that depend on several current rows.
Revision checks reject stale changes.
A failed transaction leaves no partial business decision.

The shared native HTTP command path uses a serializable transaction and a transaction advisory lock.
It can replay a retained response or execute and retain one new response atomically.
This guarantee belongs to that command path, not automatically to every piece of code in the repository.

### A separate delivery attempt

A database transaction cannot atomically commit an email provider's behavior.
The system therefore retains delivery work with the business decision, then attempts delivery separately.

The envelope describes the committed work. A worker claims it and calls the configured provider.
An acknowledgement or recoverable failure records the attempt's outcome.
A delivery retry must not repeat the original business decision.

Provider acknowledgement does not prove that a human read an email.
A local capture endpoint does not prove delivery through the future production provider.

The Bun composition starts configured workers.
Recruitment has a supervised worker with stale-claim recovery.
Other paths, including password recovery and receipt reconciliation, have explicit drain programs.
The existence of these programs does not prove unattended production operation.

### Private bytes have their own custody

For receipts, PostgreSQL holds metadata and references. Private storage holds the file bytes.
The system checks authorized access, safe paths, and file custody. Knowledge of a path is not authority.

SQL and file storage do not share one transaction.
Failed file promotion remains visible as pending reconciliation rather than a false success.
Payment-account fields use authenticated encryption with receipt-specific context.

A claim, an approval, and a settlement are separate facts.
Settlement requires separate evidence and authority. A legacy “refunded” flag cannot establish native payment evidence.

## 8. The runtime connects the implementations

`apps/backend/src/main.ts` is the native backend's composition root.
It connects the HTTP process, database, authentication, business services, file storage, and configured delivery programs.

The selected backend is portable Bun with PostgreSQL.
There is no Cloudflare Worker backend composition.
Hyperdrive transport conflicts with the PostgreSQL advisory-lock requirements of the native system.
Provider selection and deployed acceptance remain separate work.

Local `just dev` starts the backend and both frontend development servers through the existing workspace tasks.
It requires a separate, dedicated loopback PostgreSQL database and a stable authentication secret.
The launcher disables external delivery.

| Tool                   | Its responsibility                                           | What success does not prove                                                    |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| devenv / Nix           | Supply the declared development executables                  | Application authority or deployment approval                                   |
| Bun / Turbo            | Run commands and coordinate workspace tasks                  | Correct operational behavior                                                   |
| Lefthook               | Run checks that a hook configuration actually declares       | That installing its binary defines those checks                                |
| Oxfmt / Oxlint / types | Check formatting, selected source rules, and type contracts  | Real-provider delivery or complete migration                                   |
| Rehearsal commands     | Exercise bounded import, application, and recovery scenarios | Current production data or operational readiness beyond the exercised scenario |

A complete restore needs the database, private files, and required secrets.
The combined local rehearsal exposed why the authentication secret must remain stable across restore.
Restoring database rows alone is not a complete application restore.

Sources: [composition root](../apps/backend/src/main.ts), [local launcher](../tools/scripts/dev.ts), [development setup](../README.md#local-native-development), [runtime composition](architecture.md#runtime-composition).

## 9. Migration preserves meaning, not PHP structure

The replacement must uphold core operational behavior.
It does not need a one-to-one copy of legacy controllers, tables, or peripheral features.

The live legacy baseline and the Symfony modernization branch in the vektorprogrammet repository are not interchangeable.
The branch is a separate modernization attempt, not an exact production snapshot.

The combined migration candidate uses the existing import boundaries:

1. **Bind the source and review evidence.** The SELECT-only source reader produces revisions and row digests. Reviews must describe that same source.
2. **Resolve identities and references.** Accepted Person mappings anchor Accounts, Organization, historical service, and current assignments.
3. **Commit the selected SQL cohorts.** The cutover command imports its selected cohorts in one transaction. A failure rolls back that SQL import.
4. **Reconcile reviewed receipts separately.** Receipt import requires ownership, department, account, and file evidence. Private-file promotion has its own recovery state.
5. **Use the imported candidate.** Imported users sign in and read permitted records through native boundaries. Recovery scenarios challenge the same candidate.

A historical relationship cannot establish current authority by inference.
Missing receipt bytes cannot disappear behind a successful SQL import.
Every selected occurrence needs a disposition: accepted, quarantined, or explicitly excluded.
Conflicts with native edits remain visible.

### What the local rehearsal establishes

The combined synthetic rehearsal exercised real MariaDB, PostgreSQL, import commands, authentication, and private files.
It used one source and one target with shared Person identities.

Replay preserved a native password change and a receipt edit.
Interrupted phases and failed file promotion recovered without an invented transaction across SQL and the filesystem.
Logical restore retained the database, private bytes, and authentication secret.

These are local observations. They are not production-provider acceptance or a power-loss simulation.
The separate historical-backup rehearsal establishes only what that older backup contains.
It cannot establish today's workload, current mailbox ownership, or current leadership.

### What remains before cutover

- An authorized, consistent current source snapshot.
- Reviewed current authority and assignment mappings.
- Private receipt files with ownership and payment-account evidence.
- An inventory and disposition of pending operational work.
- Acceptance of the selected hosting, mail, storage, recovery, and supervision paths.
- A fenced legacy writer and an accounted final delta.
- Rollback that preserves native-only writes and pending effects.
- Explicit operator authority to transfer production responsibility.

Exact replay is not incremental synchronization.
A changed source snapshot needs an explicit reconciliation strategy, not an unqualified rerun of the original importer.
Restarting PHP after native writes is not automatically a safe rollback.

## 10. Follow one visit through all the layers

Consider an illustrative school visit. These are not a live school's records.

1. **Plan.** A coordinator records demand and confirms a reviewed roster. The server retains the plan and queues notifications.
2. **Make a dated commitment.** The coordinator selects the school, interval, and required demand. Scheduled assistants come from the confirmed roster.
3. **Handle an absence.** A scheduled volunteer reports an absence. A coordinator offers coverage to an eligible substitute. Acceptance and acknowledgement remain separate facts.
4. **Record attendance.** After the visit, the coordinator records evidence. The dashboard submits a typed command with its revision and command identity.
5. **Check authority.** The backend authenticates the person and resolves current scope inside the transaction.
6. **Apply the business rule.** The service checks the requested outcome against attendance and demand.
7. **Commit and show the result.** The transaction retains the outcome and evidence. The dashboard displays the server result.

The full chain is human intent, typed request, current authority, legal transition, durable evidence, and an updated screen.

<details>
<summary>What if two coordinators act at once?</summary>

Transactions, locking, and revision checks prevent conflicting terminal decisions.
A stale client must refresh rather than overwrite the accepted outcome.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What if the provider fails after the decision commits?</summary>

The business fact remains. Delivery failure stays visible and recoverable.
The worker retries the retained effect rather than repeat the original decision.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What if too few assistants attended?</summary>

The system cannot label the commitment Completed without sufficient attendance evidence.
Unfulfilled records unmet demand and actual attendance instead of inventing success.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What if an imported former leader signs in?</summary>

Authentication can succeed without current coordinator authority.
Historical leadership alone cannot authorize the command.

</details>
